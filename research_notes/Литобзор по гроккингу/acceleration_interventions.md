# Grokking acceleration and representation interventions during training (2022–2026), positioned against F4

Context for the writer: F4 is a reproduction-based finding on Nanda et al. 2023 (1-layer transformer, a+b mod 113, AdamW wd=1, 30% train). One-shot projection of W_E[:113] at epoch 100 onto {const, cos/sin} of its own top-4 Fourier frequencies (detected at that moment) gives a median 18x reduction in epochs to 99% test accuracy (5/5 seeds); norm-only shrink 1.2x; random-4-frequency one-shot projection 7.7x (own top-4 faster than random in 5/5); re-projection every step about 25x for both own and random frequencies; keeping top-9/20 SVD directions or a random 9-dim subspace every step gives no grokking in 15k epochs. Right after projection train accuracy falls 43% -> 3% while test loss falls 7.44 -> 5.31.

Reading legend: "[full text]" means numbers were read from the arXiv HTML full text; "[abstract-only]" means only the arXiv abstract page was read, and claims are as stated by the authors without verified numbers. Speed-ups across papers are NOT comparable: tasks, moduli, data fractions, thresholds (95% vs 99%), and units (steps vs epochs, full-batch vs mini-batch) differ.

## Q1. Grokfast (Lee et al. 2024, arXiv 2405.20233): mechanism, speed-ups, settings; follow-ups and critiques

### Takeaway
Grokfast low-pass filters the gradient sequence (moving average or EMA) and adds lambda times the slow component to the gradient; its headline "x50" is iterations-to-95%-val on modular multiplication mod 97 with a 2-layer transformer and requires co-tuned weight decay; without weight decay the best gain was x13.6, and an independent comparison (NeuralGrok) found Grokfast giving roughly 1x on several mod-97 tasks at 50% data. It acts on optimizer dynamics for all parameters every step, not on the representation directly.

### Cited Findings
- Authors: Jaerin Lee, Bong Gyun Kang, Kihoon Kim, Kyoung Mu Lee; arXiv preprint, v1 30 May 2024, v2 5 Jun 2024; code github.com/ironjr/grokfast. The abs page lists no venue. — [arXiv 2405.20233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20233)
- Mechanism: treat each parameter's gradient history as a time signal, split into a fast-varying component (linked to overfitting) and a slow-varying component (linked to generalization), and amplify the slow one. Grokfast-MA: average of the last w gradients, added with weight lambda; Grokfast-EMA: EMA with momentum alpha, added with weight lambda. Recommended lambda 0.1–5, alpha 0.8–0.99. [full text] — [Grokfast HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20233v2)
- Modular multiplication mod 97, 2-layer transformer, iterations to 95% val acc: baseline 39,890; Grokfast-MA (lambda=5, w=100, no wd) 2,940 (x13.57); two-stage MA (switched on after about 500 its) 1,920 (x20.78); Grokfast-MA + wd=0.01 790 (x50.49; wall-clock x20.49 because MA costs about 2.4x per iteration, 0.37 s vs 0.15 s); Grokfast-EMA (alpha=0.98, lambda=2, wd=0.005) 910 (x43.84; wall-clock x43.79). [full text] — [Grokfast HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20233v2)
- MNIST (1,000-image subset, 3-layer ReLU MLP, large-init grokking setup): EMA alpha=0.8, lambda=0.1, wd=2.0, iterations 44,022 -> 2,001 (x22.0), final acc 89.8% -> 91.2%. QM9 (100 samples, GCN) and IMDb (1,000 rows, 2-layer LSTM): no speed-up factor given, only lower min val loss (QM9 0.00659 -> 0.00348; IMDb val acc 0.84 -> 0.90). [full text] — [Grokfast HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20233v2)
- Stated limitations: Grokfast-MA memory/compute cost; slow gradients alone (without the raw gradient) were unstable and did not converge; strong hyperparameter "sweet spot" sensitivity; wd alone made training unstable in their setup; experiments use tiny data subsets. [full text] — [Grokfast HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20233v2)
- Independent comparison (NeuralGrok, Zhou, Fan, Jaggi, Fu, arXiv 2504.17243, Table 1; 2-layer transformers, mod 97, 50/50 split, steps to 95% test): a+b standard 1,650 / Grokfast-MA 1,780 / Grokfast-EMA 1,820 / NeuralGrok 900; a-b 4,330 / 2,990 / 1,340 / 1,467; a*b 1,280 / 1,150 / 1,400 / 918; a^2-b 4,820 / 3,830 / 2,730 / 1,845; composite ac+bd-e mod 7 (4-layer): standard and Grokfast-EMA did not reach 95% within 1e6 steps, Grokfast-MA 8,853, NeuralGrok 1,896 (4.67x vs MA). Single runs, no seed variance. NeuralGrok learns an MLP gradient transformation by bilevel optimization. [full text] — [NeuralGrok HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17243v2)
- Follow-ups/critiques (preprints, snippets or abstracts only): "The Order Is The Message" (arXiv 2603.25047) frames Grokfast as a low-pass filter on the gradient sequence that accelerates exit from memorization but does not eliminate it, and argues a data-ordering account explains the delay [snippet-only] — [arXiv 2603.25047](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25047); Egalitarian Gradient Descent (Saheb Pasand, Dohmatob, arXiv 2510.04930, v3 May 2026) attributes grokking to unequal speeds along principal gradient directions and normalizes them to equal speed, on modular addition and sparse parity, claiming "groks much faster" and sometimes removing the plateau, no numbers on the abs page [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2510.04930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04930); a search snippet says EGD presents itself as related to a simplified Grokfast, which I could not verify on the abstract page — [EGD HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04930v1).
- Other optimizer-level accelerators: Muon vs AdamW (Tveit et al., arXiv 2504.16041) mean grokking epoch 153.09 -> 102.89 (p about 6.3e-8) on modular arithmetic with matched wd [search snippet] — [arXiv 2504.16041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16041); Wang (arXiv 2607.20512, Jul 2026) attributes Muon's speed-up to Newton–Schulz orthogonalization, not the spectral-norm constraint, reports orthogonalizing optimizers generalize at about 3x lower spectral norm, and warns that "faster" can invert under a sustained-grok metric [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2607.20512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20512); arXiv 2608.07436 reports Muon median 5,400 vs AdamW 8,300 steps (1.54x) with post-grokking collapse [search snippet] — [arXiv 2608.07436](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07436).
- "Grokking at the edge of numerical stability" (Prieto, Barsbey, Mediano, Birdal, arXiv 2501.04697, v2 May 2025): Softmax Collapse (floating-point errors when logits grow) blocks grokking without regularization; after overfitting the gradient aligns with "naive loss minimization" (scaling logits along current weight direction); StableMax enables grokking without regularization; ⊥Grad removes the NLM component for "quick generalization". No speed-up numbers on the abs page. [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2501.04697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04697)

### Inferences
- Grokfast's x50 is measured on a different task (multiplication mod 97, 2-layer, Power et al. style mini-batch steps, 95% val), and is strongly hyperparameter- and wd-dependent; NeuralGrok's re-run shows near-zero gain from Grokfast at 50% data. F4's 18x (epochs to 99%, mod 113, 30% data, wd=1, 5 seeds, pre-registered) is therefore in the same order of magnitude as published accelerators but should not be compared numerically.
- Grokfast/EGD/Muon/⊥Grad change the optimizer for all parameters at every step; F4 is a single, one-time edit of one weight matrix (W_E) and the rest of training is unmodified AdamW. That is a qualitatively different lever (representation surgery vs gradient filtering).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed critique of Grokfast found; the venue of Grokfast is not shown on arXiv (I did not verify any later conference acceptance).
- ⊥Grad and EGD speed-up numbers not retrieved (abstract pages only).

## Q2. Representation-level methods: GrokTransfer, embedding-layer mechanisms, KA transfer, grokking tickets, Omnigrok, Fourier/spherical priors, low-rank and projection constraints, wd schedules, 2025–2026 papers

### Takeaway
The embedding is repeatedly identified as the bottleneck (GrokTransfer, 2505.15624), and the strongest representation-level accelerations come from (a) transplanting an informative embedding from another trained model (GrokTransfer, about 10x on mod-113 addition for the FNN target), (b) training a sparse subnetwork whose mask is taken from the already-generalized model (grokking tickets), or (c) architectural priors such as spherical normalization (>20x, abstract). Generic subspace confinement of training dynamics is reported to prevent grokking (2602.16746), consistent with F4's failed SVD/random-9-dim constraints.

### Cited Findings
- GrokTransfer (Xu, Ni, Wang, Hu; ICLR 2025; arXiv 2504.13292): train a small weak model to "nontrivial but far from optimal" test accuracy, take its input embedding, initialize the target embedding as E_T = A·B with A from the weak embedding and B random, both trainable. Proven on synthetic XOR to remove delayed generalization. — [arXiv 2504.13292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13292)
- GrokTransfer setups: modular addition p=113 with 25% train, modular multiplication, (40,3)-parity; targets are a 3-layer FNN and an 8-layer transformer (d_embed=512, d_mlp=512, 4 heads); weak model for modular tasks is a 2-layer NN trained 1e4 epochs; for FNN->transformer the FNN embedding is taken when it first reaches 30% test acc. Reported: on p=113 addition the target from scratch takes about 10,000 epochs vs about 1,000 with GrokTransfer (text, about 10x implied); FNN->transformer wall-clock weak 28 ms + target 71,079 ms vs scratch 392,667 ms ("approximately five times faster"); vs GrokFast on a 2-layer transformer, "time gap" 46 vs 1,119. Weak-model test accuracy correlates with the target's eventual test accuracy. Init-scale tuning alone does not remove grokking (App. A.3.1). Section 2.1: fixed Fourier embedding with k=7 frequencies chosen as the 7 smallest primes (not the model's own), norm 1, avoids the sharp transition and lets memorization and generalization happen together. Fig. 15 has a random low-rank A·B control (merged into the embedding at epoch 100), but its numerical result was not readable in the text. [full text, figures not readable] — [GrokTransfer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13292)
- "Mechanistic Insights into Grokking from the Embedding Layer" (AlQuabeh, Bojkovic, Nwadike, Inui; arXiv 2505.15624, May 2025, no venue shown): adding embeddings to MLPs creates grokking on modular arithmetic while embedding-free MLPs generalize immediately; mechanisms are (1) sparse embedding updates for rarely sampled tokens plus wd, (2) bilinear coupling between embeddings and downstream weights creating saddle points. Fixes: frequency-aware (uniform) token sampling, and an embedding-specific learning rate with ratio eta_E/eta_W ∝ (sigma_max(E)/sigma_max(W))·(f_W/f_E); Adam-LR uses 10x embedding LR. Setup: P=97, 2-layer ReLU MLP, embed dim 128, Adam wd=0.001. Only qualitative speed-up claims ("significant acceleration"), no epoch counts, no Grokfast comparison. DFT of the learned embedding shows clear frequency peaks for addition/multiplication, diffuse for division. [full text] — [arXiv 2505.15624 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15624)
- Kolmogorov–Arnold view (Park, Kim, Kim; arXiv 2405.16658, May 2024, no venue shown): data augmentation for commutative ops plus transfer learning based on shared KA structure across binary operations (embedding transfer), including learning ops from a limited number of tokens; no speed-up numbers in the abstract. [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2405.16658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16658)
- Grokking tickets (Minegishi, Iwasawa, Matsuo; TMLR; arXiv 2310.19470 v3 May 2025): magnitude-prune (rate 0.6 default) the dense network at epoch t >= t_gen (i.e., after generalization), rewind survivors to init, retrain. Tickets from before about 25k epochs ("non-grokked tickets") do not fully generalize. Setup: MLP on mod-67 addition, 40% train, embed 500, 48 hidden ReLU, AdamW lr 1e-3 wd 1.0; also a 1-layer 1-head transformer. Controls: dense model rescaled so its L1/L2 norm matches the ticket still generalizes much slower; random pruning and pruning-at-init (SNIP, GraSP, SynFlow) at matched sparsity give no speed-up. Tickets develop periodic (low Fourier-entropy) weights by about 2k epochs vs about 30k for the base model. Edge-popup (mask search, weights frozen, from memorized solution) reaches 0.92 test acc by epoch 2000. Exact base-vs-ticket epoch numbers only in figures. [full text] — [arXiv 2310.19470 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19470)
- Omnigrok (Liu, Michaud, Tegmark; ICLR 2023; arXiv 2210.01117): "LU mechanism" (train loss L-shaped, test loss U-shaped vs weight norm); large init norm causes grokking; training with constrained weight norm "can almost eliminate" grokking; secondary summary says the norm was enforced by rescaling after each step. — [arXiv 2210.01117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01117); [LessWrong summary](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/jusq6kyZ6XSrtW3Bf/paper-summary-omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data)
- Weight norm vs logit scale (Truong, arXiv 2606.18465, Jun 2026, single author, not peer reviewed): with weight norm clamped, varying only output temperature slides the grokking delay across the whole norm-induced range; delay collapses onto logit scale (R^2=0.97), norm adds 1–2%; under MSE the picture differs. [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2606.18465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18465)
- Spherical/Fourier architectural prior (Yıldırım, arXiv 2603.05228, v3 May 2026): L2-normalized residual stream plus fixed-temperature unembedding cuts grokking onset by "over 20x" without wd on cyclic modular addition; uniform-attention ablation reaches 100% across seeds; no acceleration on non-commutative S5. Search snippets say Fourier-initialized weights are part of the configuration. [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2603.05228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05228)
- Dissection of that configuration (Hampton, Khan, Khan; arXiv 2611.03156, Nov 2026): 960-run factorial; with wd, runs without residual projection took 8–37x longer to cross; projection alone did not induce grokking; rapid grokking occurred without Fourier init ("Fourier initialization insufficient to explain"); snippet: adding Fourier init in one configuration gave 720 ± 215 epochs (10/10 crossings); geometry and output scale could not be separated; acceleration not uniform across tasks/depths; faster first crossing did not imply persistence. [abstract + snippet] — [arXiv 2611.03156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03156)
- Subspace confinement (Yongzhong Xu, arXiv 2602.16746, v3 Apr 2026): one PCA component captures 68–83% of attention-weight trajectory variance; suppressing gradient flow orthogonal to the learned execution subspace prevents generalization with monotonic dose-response across four operations; boosting curvature defects does nothing. Search snippet: full projection (s=1.0) completely prevents generalization. [abstract + snippet] — [arXiv 2602.16746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16746)
- Low-rank decay (arXiv 2606.04405, 2026): a regularizer that collapses effective rank of Q/K matrices; snippet says it expands the data-fraction boundary at which delayed generalization occurs. [snippet-only] — [arXiv 2606.04405](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04405)
- Weight-decay schedules (arXiv 2607.20552, "Thermodynamic Weight Decay"): continuous proportional wd schedule vs step spike; single-seed favorable, multi-seed "suggestive but mixed". [snippet-only] — [arXiv 2607.20552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20552)
- Frequency lottery (He, Wang, Chen, Yang; arXiv 2602.16849, Feb 2026): in 2-layer nets on modular addition, frequencies compete within each neuron and the winner is set by its initial spectral magnitude and phase alignment; grokking is a 3-stage process driven by loss vs wd competition. [abstract-only] — [arXiv 2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16849)
- Key frequencies of Nanda-style mod-113 reproductions differ by seed (e.g. {5, 9, 17, 28, 43} in one reproduction) — [GitHub reproduction](https://github.com/jenil-dobariya22/Grokking-on-Modular-Addition-Fourier-Circuit-Analysis-in-a-1-Layer-Transformer)

### Inferences
- Layer swapping: no paper found on "layer swapping" to accelerate grokking (search returned nothing).
- GrokTransfer, KA transfer, and 2505.15624 all point at the same lever as F4: the input embedding. F4 adds an intervention that needs no auxiliary model, no final-model mask, and no architectural change.
- The 2602.16746 confinement result and F4's every-step SVD/random-9-dim failure are consistent: forcing the dynamics into a fixed generic subspace can block generalization, whereas F4's every-step Fourier projection does not block it. That makes "Fourier-ness of the subspace" (not low dimension per se) the variable F4 isolates.

### Gaps
- Exact epoch counts for GrokTransfer per figure, its random low-rank control (Fig. 15) and the 7-prime Fourier embedding curve were not readable in text.
- Spherical paper: modulus and whether the Fourier init used task-specific frequencies were not confirmed.

## Q3. Which prior result is closest to F4, and how F4 differs

### Takeaway
The closest prior results are GrokTransfer (an informative embedding injected into training, including a fixed Fourier embedding with arbitrary prime frequencies) and grokking tickets (structure taken from the model itself, with a norm-matched control). F4 differs on three axes neither paper covers together: the frequencies are self-detected from the same run at epoch 100 (no auxiliary model, no post-generalization information), the edit is a one-shot projection mid-training rather than an initialization, and it has explicit norm-only and random-frequency controls showing direction, not norm, carries most of the effect (18x vs 1.2x) and that the model's own frequencies beat random ones (18x vs 7.7x, 5/5).

### Cited Findings
- GrokTransfer requires a separately trained weak model and initializes the target embedding from it (A·B factorization, both trainable); speed-up about 10x on p=113 addition (FNN target) and about 5x wall-clock FNN->transformer. — [GrokTransfer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13292)
- GrokTransfer's own Fourier-embedding baseline uses 7 frequencies = the 7 smallest primes, i.e., not the model's own frequencies, and it removes the sharp transition. — [GrokTransfer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13292)
- Grokking tickets take masks only at t >= t_gen (after generalization); tickets from earlier epochs do not fully generalize; norm-matched dense control generalizes slower; random pruning gives no gain. — [arXiv 2310.19470 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19470)
- Grokfast does not touch representations directly: it filters gradients of all parameters every step (up to x50 on mod-97 multiplication with wd). — [Grokfast HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.20233v2)
- 2505.15624 changes embedding learning dynamics (sampling, LR ratio) but not the embedding's content. — [arXiv 2505.15624 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15624)
- Frequency winners are set early by initial spectral magnitude/phase (lottery mechanism), which offers a reason the model's own top-4 at epoch 100 are good choices. — [arXiv 2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16849)

### Inferences
- F4 vs grokking tickets: tickets need information from after generalization (t_gen); F4 uses only epoch-100 information. Both use a norm-matched control and both find norm is not the main factor (F4: 1.2x for norm-only). But tickets report random pruning gives no gain, while F4's random-frequency projection still gives 7.7x: in F4 the generic Fourier basis itself is informative, and the model's own frequency selection adds about 2.3x on top (18/7.7).
- F4 vs GrokTransfer: GrokTransfer's "weak model" is a different, smaller network trained to partial generalization; F4's "weak model" is the same network at epoch 100, and the transferred object is not the embedding matrix but only its Fourier support (4 frequencies), with amplitudes and phases re-fit by projection. This is the strongest novelty claim.
- F4's train-accuracy crash (43% -> 3%) together with test-loss drop (7.44 -> 5.31) is consistent with the projection deleting memorization stored in non-key frequencies, in line with descriptions of grokking as weight decay pruning non-feature frequencies (snippet in Q4 search) and with Nanda-style restricted/excluded-loss logic. This is an inference, not a cited result.
- Given 2606.18465 (logit scale, not norm, controls delay under CE), F4's norm-only control (W_E shrunk to the same norm, 1.2x) is a useful but partial control: it does not hold the logit scale fixed. A logit-scale-matched control would strengthen the claim that direction (Fourier support), not output scale, drives the 18x.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that projects or prunes the embedding mid-training onto self-detected Fourier frequencies, or that uses only early-training frequency information to intervene; this is a negative search result over arXiv/web search, not proof of absence.

## Q4. Has anyone shown that imposing a random (not the model's own) Fourier subspace speeds grokking, or that generic low-rank (SVD) constraints fail?

### Takeaway
Partially: GrokTransfer's fixed Fourier embedding with arbitrary prime frequencies (not the model's own) removes the sharp grokking transition in an FNN, which is the closest published analogue to F4's random-frequency result, but it is an initialization/fixed embedding, not a mid-training projection, and no speed-up number was readable. For generic low-rank/subspace constraints, the closest evidence is that confining gradient flow to a learned PCA subspace prevents grokking (2602.16746); I found no paper testing top-k SVD of the embedding or a random k-dim subspace as F4 does.

### Cited Findings
- Fixed Fourier embedding, 7 smallest-prime frequencies, norm 1, avoids the sharp transition (FNN, modular addition). — [GrokTransfer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13292)
- Spherical/Fourier configuration accelerates >20x, but dissection shows Fourier init is neither necessary nor sufficient; residual projection matters more and interacts with wd and output scale. — [arXiv 2603.05228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05228); [arXiv 2611.03156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03156)
- A search summary of a sinusoidal-activation paper (arXiv 2511.23443) says earlier work showed Fourier-style embeddings accelerate modular-addition learning and reduce grokking [snippet-only, primary not checked]. — [arXiv 2511.23443](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.23443)
- Suppressing orthogonal-to-learned-subspace gradient flow prevents generalization with monotonic dose-response. — [arXiv 2602.16746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16746)
- Low-rank Q/K decay widens the delayed-generalization regime rather than shortening it [snippet-only]. — [arXiv 2606.04405](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04405)
- GrokTransfer has a random low-rank A·B embedding control merged at epoch 100 (Fig. 15); its outcome was not readable. — [GrokTransfer HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13292)
- Random pruning at matched sparsity gives no speed-up vs base (grokking tickets). — [arXiv 2310.19470 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19470)

### Inferences
- F4's triplet (own Fourier 18x, random Fourier 7.7x one-shot / about 25x every step, SVD top-9 or random 9-dim every step = no grokking) separates three hypotheses that the literature mixes: low rank alone (fails), Fourier basis in general (works, even random frequencies), and the model's own frequencies (best for a one-shot edit). The every-step result (own approx. random approx. 25x) suggests that once the embedding is continuously held in any small Fourier subspace, the downstream MLP can build the algorithm on whichever frequencies it is given, consistent with the clock algorithm working for any frequency set.
- For the write-up: the "random Fourier helps" result is supported in spirit by GrokTransfer's prime-frequency embedding but, to my search, not previously reported as a mid-training intervention with a matched own-vs-random comparison over seeds.

### Gaps
- Could not read GrokTransfer Fig. 15 (random low-rank control) and Fig. 2 (Fourier embedding) numbers.
- Did not find a published test of top-k SVD projection of W_E during training; 2602.16746 constrains the trajectory of attention weights, not the embedding.
- arXiv 2511.23443 and 2609.38453 (minimum-norm interpolation, sparsity bias) were only seen as search snippets.
