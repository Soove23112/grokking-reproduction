## Q1. Founding works: what algorithm do networks learn for modular arithmetic?

### Takeaway
The consensus, from Nanda et al. 2023 onward, is a Fourier/"clock" algorithm: embed tokens as cos/sin at a handful of key frequencies, multiply them (attention + MLP) to get cos/sin(w(a+b)), and read out logits via cos(w(a+b−c)). Each MLP neuron ends up driven by one frequency. Later work generalizes this to group representations (Chughtai et al.), shows an alternative "pizza" family (Zhong et al.), and since 2025 argues these are surface variants of one abstract algorithm (McCracken et al. "approximate CRT"; Moisescu-Pareja et al. manifolds).

### Cited Findings
- Power et al. 2022 introduce grokking on small algorithmic datasets (binary operation tables), showing generalization long after overfitting, with weight decay among the most effective interventions; arXiv 2201.02177, ICLR 2022 MATH-AI workshop [MEM] — [arXiv:2201.02177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177)
- Nanda, Chan, Lieberum, Smith, Steinhardt 2023 (ICLR 2023): 1-layer transformer, p=113, 30% data, full-batch AdamW, wd=1; the network embeds inputs at a few (5 in the main run) key frequencies and uses trig identities to compute cos(w(a+b−c)); define restricted loss / excluded loss progress measures; split training into memorization (~0–1.4k epochs), circuit formation (~1.4k–9.4k) and cleanup (~9.4k–13.3k), with test accuracy jumping at cleanup; key frequencies are already being built in during "circuit formation", i.e. well before the test jump [MEM] — [arXiv:2301.05217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217). Earlier version: Nanda & Lieberum, "A Mechanistic Interpretability Analysis of Grokking", Alignment Forum, Aug 2022 [MEM] — [AF post](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/N6WM6hs7RQMKDhYjB/a-mechanistic-interpretability-analysis-of-grokking)
- Chughtai, Chan, Nanda 2023 (ICML 2023), "A Toy Model of Universality": for general finite groups networks learn a group-representation ("GCR") algorithm, of which the Fourier algorithm is the cyclic-group case; which representations are learned varies across random seeds ("weak universality") [MEM] — [arXiv:2302.03025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03025)
- Stander, Yu, Fan, Biderman 2023/24, "Grokking Group Multiplication with Cosets": on S5/S6 the networks implement a coset-based algorithm, challenging the GCR reading [MEM] — [arXiv:2312.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06581)
- Wu et al. 2024, "Unifying and Verifying Mechanistic Interpretations: A Case Study with Group Operations": reconciles GCR vs coset readings with causal verification [MEM] — [arXiv:2410.07476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07476)
- Gromov 2023, "Grokking modular arithmetic": analytic weights for a 2-layer MLP with quadratic activation; each neuron is periodic at one frequency with input phases φ_a, φ_b and output phase φ_a+φ_b; random phases across neurons do the "voting" [MEM] — [arXiv:2301.02679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02679)
- Liu, Kitouni, Nolte, Michaud, Tegmark, Williams 2022 (NeurIPS 2022), "Towards Understanding Grokking: An Effective Theory of Representation Learning": embeddings form a ring/structured representation; phase diagram memorization / grokking / comprehension / confusion vs hyperparameters [MEM] — [arXiv:2205.10343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10343); Omnigrok (ICLR 2023) ties grokking to weight norm — [arXiv:2210.01117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01117)
- Morwani, Edelman, Oncescu, Zhao, Kakade 2023 (ICLR 2024), "Feature emergence via margin maximization": max-margin solutions of 1-hidden-layer nets on modular addition (and other group tasks) provably use Fourier features with each neuron at a single frequency; this explains the *final* structure, not the dynamics [MEM] — [arXiv:2311.07568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07568)
- Furuta, Minegishi, Iwasawa, Matsuo 2024, "Interpreting Grokked Transformers in Complex Modular Arithmetic": extends Fourier analysis to subtraction, multiplication, polynomials; subtraction/asymmetric ops and polynomials give less sparse / harder Fourier structure; propose Fourier-sparsity progress measures [MEM] — [arXiv:2402.16726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16726)
- Doshi, Das, He, Gromov: "To grok or not to grok" (corrupted labels; ICLR 2024) [MEM] — [arXiv:2310.13061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13061); "Grokking modular polynomials" [MEM] — [arXiv:2406.03495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03495); "Grokking vs. Learning: Same features, different encodings" (2025): grokked and steadily trained nets converge on the same Fourier features for modular addition, but steady training encodes them more compactly; neurons respond to sin/cos of a single frequency [ABS/search snippet] — [arXiv:2502.01739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01739)
- McCracken, Moisescu-Pareja, Letourneau, Precup, Love 2025, "Uncovering a Universal Abstract Algorithm for Modular Addition": MLPs and transformers implement an "approximate Chinese Remainder Theorem"; neurons fire on "approximate cosets"; with trainable embeddings or >1 hidden layer only O(log n) features are needed, confirmed empirically [ABS] — [arXiv:2505.18266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18266)
- Recurrent nets: an RNN with untied embedding/unembedding trained on modular addition shows low-rank, sparse Fourier structure, with the same 6 frequencies in every tensor examined [ABS/search snippet] — [arXiv:2503.22059](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22059v1)
- ReLU MLPs (Swaroop 2026): input weights are near-binary square waves rather than sinusoids; output phases ≈ φ_a+φ_b; ideal-wave reconstruction from DFT-extracted frequencies/phases reaches 95.8% vs 0.40% for the noisy source models; relations persist in non-grokking and label-noise models; "grokking sharpens an algorithm already encoded during memorization" [ABS] — [arXiv:2603.23784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23784)
- Mechanistic explanations of *why* grokking is delayed (not frequency-specific): circuit efficiency (Varma et al. 2023) [MEM] — [arXiv:2309.02390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02390); lazy→rich transition (Kumar et al., ICLR 2024) [MEM] — [arXiv:2310.06110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06110); early/late implicit-bias dichotomy (Lyu et al., ICLR 2024) [MEM] — [arXiv:2311.18817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18817); kernel-regime impossibility for modular addition (Mohamadi et al., ICML 2024) [MEM] — [arXiv:2407.12332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12332); Tian 2024 "Composing global optimizers" (CoGO), algebraic construction of global optima of 2-layer nets on modular addition from per-frequency partial solutions [MEM] — [arXiv:2410.01779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01779)

### Inferences
- Nanda et al. already established that the circuit is built gradually during "circuit formation" before the test jump; any "early selection" finding is therefore a refinement of their timeline (resolution: when, and in which weight matrix), not a contradiction.
- The 2025–2026 universality line (approximate CRT, manifold equivalence) reframes "which frequencies" as "which of many interchangeable coset/frequency choices", consistent with symmetry: for prime p, the automorphism a→k·a of Z_p maps frequency 1 to any frequency k, so under one-hot inputs no frequency is a priori privileged; selection must come from symmetry breaking by init and by the training subset.

### Gaps
- Exact phase boundaries and seed-to-seed frequency variability in Nanda et al. 2023 were not re-verified in this session (marked [MEM]).
- Full texts of 2502.01739, 2505.18266, 2603.23784 were not read; their timing/frequency-count details are abstract-level only.

## Q2. Training dynamics: how and when do frequencies emerge and get selected? (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
2025–2026 theory (He et al. lottery ticket; Kunin et al. alternating gradient flows; Wang et al. probability signatures; Tan et al. classifier-first geometry) treats frequencies as approximately decoupled Fourier modes that compete, with the winner set by initial magnitude (and, per He et al., phase misalignment), followed by weight-decay pruning. Almost all of this is for 2-layer MLPs with small init and often quadratic activation; time-resolved measurements in Nanda's 1-layer transformer are rare, and the closest one is a GitHub write-up, not a paper.

### Cited Findings
- He, Wang, Chen, Yang 2026, "On the Mechanism and Dynamics of Modular Addition: Fourier Features, Lottery Ticket, and Grokking" (arXiv 2602.16849, Feb 2026; also on OpenReview) [FULL, first ~40% of HTML] — [arXiv:2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16849v1); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Qc4RLl7t3S); [code](https://github.com/Y-Agent/modular-addition-feature-learning)
  - Setup: 2-layer MLP, M=512 hidden neurons, no biases, p=23; experiments with ReLU, theory with quadratic activation and small init κ_init under gradient flow; AdamW lr 1e-4; main experiments on full data; grokking experiments at train fraction 0.75, wd=2.0.
  - Lottery ticket: frequencies compete within each neuron; "the one with the largest α_mk(0) and β_mk(0) and the smallest D̃_mk(0) ultimately wins", where α, β are input/output magnitudes at frequency k and D̃ = max{D, 2π−D}, D = (2φ_mk − ψ_mk) mod 2π (phase misalignment between input phase φ and output phase ψ).
  - Validation is qualitative: one neuron's trajectories plus a contour plot of final magnitude over (initial magnitude, initial misalignment) after 10,000 steps; no percentage of neurons whose winner is correctly predicted was found in the part read.
  - Phase coupling over training: output phase converges to twice the input phase (2φ−ψ → 0) monotonically, "without crossing the axis", faster for smaller initial misalignment; proved for the quadratic case (Sec. 5.5).
  - Global structure: "diversification condition" = frequency diversification (equal neuron count per frequency) + phase symmetry + magnitude balance, giving a majority-vote that cancels per-neuron noise; with p=23, M=512 all 11 frequencies appear across neurons. They say frequency balance is an idealization that holds approximately under random init.
  - Grokking stages: memorization (train acc peaks by ~step 1000), generalization stage 1 (weight decay prunes remaining non-winning components, test loss drops sharply; turning point ~step 10,000), stage 2 (weight decay slowly pushes test accuracy to 100%). Repo README phrases it as memorization → sparsification → cleanup.
- Kunin, Marchetti, Chen, Karkada, Simon, DeWeese, Ganguli, Miolane 2025 (NeurIPS 2025), "Alternating Gradient Flows": from small init, gradient flow is a staircase alternating between dormant-neuron alignment (utility maximization) and active-neuron growth; for quadratic nets on modular addition this gives "the first complete characterization of the training dynamics", with Fourier features learned "in decreasing order of coefficient magnitude" [ABS] — [arXiv:2506.06489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06489)
- Wang, Yao, Liu, Lu, Xu 2026, "Probability-Signature Dynamics": leading gradient interactions are conditional statistics of the training distribution; for modular addition these are cyclic shift operators diagonalized by the DFT, giving approximately decoupled per-Fourier-mode dynamics that explain Fourier sparsity, frequency matching (input vs output) and phase alignment; also explains why label noise can speed early loss decrease (submitted to ICLR 2027) [ABS] — [arXiv:2610.11833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11833)
- Tan, Gai, Zhang 2026, "Beyond Neural Collapse: Task-Intrinsic Geometry Governs Neural Representations in Modular Arithmetic": classifier (output) weights reach a rank-2 equiangular configuration first, then embeddings reorganize into that plane; within it embedding formation reduces to phase alignment on a circle whose minimizers are single-frequency characters; weight decay suppresses off-plane components; critical wd threshold Θ(1/K) [ABS] — [arXiv:2606.08985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08985)
- "Neural Networks Provably Learn Spectral Representations for Group Composition" (2026): over groups like Z3×Z5 the surviving frequencies are spread roughly uniformly across the non-trivial frequencies [search snippet only] — [arXiv:2606.02993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02993)
- "A Counterexample to Fourier Alignment in Single-Neuron Modular Addition" (2026): a single ReLU neuron trained with full-table cross-entropy and no weight decay does not necessarily become single-frequency, so single-frequency alignment is not a general consequence of training [search snippet only] — [arXiv:2608.04451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04451)
- Moisescu-Pareja, McCracken et al. (Dec 2025; ICLR 2026): learned phase alignment between the two inputs in first-layer preactivations shapes intermediate representations [search snippet only] — [arXiv:2512.25060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.25060)
- Swaroop 2026: algorithmic (frequency/phase) structure is already present in memorizing and non-grokking models; grokking "sharpens" it [ABS] — [arXiv:2603.23784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23784)
- Embedding-side regularization: an ICML 2026 paper applies ℓ1 in Fourier space to (un)embedding layers and reports bypassing grokking with up to 3× faster training; related operations in a group share the same Fourier spectrum [search snippet only] — [ICML 2026 poster 79490](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/79490). "Mechanistic Insights into Grokking from the Embedding Layer" (2025) [title/snippet only] — [arXiv:2505.15624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15624)
- eNTK-based feature identification places the sparse key-frequency emergence around the grokking transition in its setup [search snippet only] — [arXiv:2510.00468](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00468)
- Closest time-resolved measurement in Nanda's exact setup (1-layer transformer, p=113, 30% split, wd=1, 10 init seeds, fixed split) is a GitHub write-up by account "Amarine007" (no author name/date; 41 commits; 2026) [REPO, README read]: — [github.com/Amarine007/grokking-dynamics](https://github.com/Amarine007/grokking-dynamics)
  - Eventual key frequencies already rank higher in the random embedding than chance: mean rank 23.6/56 vs 28.5 ± 2.3 for random picks (one-sided permutation p=0.016); only 14 of 47 final frequencies start in the top ten; on the neuron-to-logit readout W_L the head start is not significant (24.9, p=0.057). Author: init "tilts the odds" but does not decide.
  - "Core" frequencies (35/47) enter the embedding's top-n for good between step 0 and step 462, before memorization ends (806–1,409 steps); "late" frequencies (12/47) lock in between 3,500 and 40,000 steps.
  - Frequencies used during circuit formation can later be dropped (in 7/10 seeds a checkpoint's own top-n set gave 11–25 points higher restricted test accuracy at some point).
  - Restricted test loss falls below ln 113 at median step 250, a median 5,750 steps before test accuracy reaches 50%.
  - In 5 seeds the readout W_L uses fewer frequencies than the embedding (≤0.01% of norm on the fifth embedding key frequency).
  - No per-neuron commitment timing; no causal "plant a frequency at init" test; core/late split chosen post hoc.
- Other small replication write-ups report embedding key-frequency energy rising from ~0.14 at epoch 500 (memorizing checkpoint) to ~0.85 at the end in a 1-layer transformer, i.e. concentration builds during memorization [REPO/search snippet only] — [jenil-dobariya22 repo](https://github.com/jenil-dobariya22/Grokking-on-Modular-Addition-Fourier-Circuit-Analysis-in-a-1-Layer-Transformer); [bushesarebetter repo](https://github.com/bushesarebetter/grokking-progress-measures)
- Dissent on embedding as the locus: Ootani 2026 (12K-param Llama-style model, multi-seed) is reported in search snippets to find per-dimension embedding Fourier concentration essentially uncorrelated with generalization, with predictive periodicity in the output map; the abstract read in this session does not state this, only that grokking is conditional/fragile across seeds and even flips with CPU thread count or CPU vs GPU [ABS; frequency claim unverified] — [arXiv:2607.05104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05104)

### Inferences
- The theory community's default picture is "per-neuron competition with init-determined winners" (He et al.) plus "modes learned in order of initial coefficient magnitude" (AGF). Both are derived for small-init, 2-layer, often quadratic MLPs with one-hot (or fixed) inputs; in Nanda's transformer the frequency is first chosen in a shared embedding W_E that every neuron reads, so a global (embedding-level) selection preceding per-neuron commitment is a natural but untested consequence.
- Tan et al. predict output-side (classifier) leads and embedding follows; the Amarine007 README finds an init head start in W_E but not in W_L. These point in different directions on which side leads, so a lead–lag measurement between W_E and W_out·W_U spectra is a genuinely open question.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper found that tracks, in the 1-layer transformer, the epoch at which W_E's top Fourier components become the final key frequencies with multi-seed statistics.
- He et al.'s remaining 60% of HTML (Sec. 6.1 Corollary 6.1 on lottery ticket under random init, and grokking-stage figures) was not read; there may be quantitative validation I did not see.

## Q3. Has anyone shown early embedding choice + late neuron commitment, or measured embedding–unembedding frequency alignment over training? (positions F1)

### Takeaway
Partly known: Nanda et al. (circuit formation precedes the jump), Swaroop 2026 (structure encoded during memorization), and especially the Amarine007 GitHub write-up (35/47 key frequencies fixed in W_E before step 462, extra frequencies dropped later) already show early embedding selection. I found no source that explicitly contrasts early embedding selection with late per-neuron single-frequency commitment, and no source that reports a time series of embedding-vs-output-side frequency-profile correlation in a transformer; the closest are He et al.'s per-neuron input/output phase coupling and Tan et al.'s "classifier first, embeddings follow".

### Cited Findings
- Circuit formation (and thus key-frequency build-up) runs from ~1.4k to ~9.4k epochs, before the test jump at cleanup [MEM] — [arXiv:2301.05217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)
- Core key frequencies enter W_E's top-n between step 0 and 462, before memorization ends; frequencies used in circuit formation can later be dropped (7/10 seeds) [REPO] — [Amarine007/grokking-dynamics](https://github.com/Amarine007/grokking-dynamics)
- "Grokking sharpens an algorithm already encoded during memorization" (ReLU MLP) [ABS] — [arXiv:2603.23784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23784)
- Weight decay prunes non-winning components in "generalization stage 1" after memorization (MLP) — [arXiv:2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16849v1)
- Per-neuron input/output phase coupling 2φ−ψ→0 converges monotonically during training (MLP, phases rather than frequency profiles) — [arXiv:2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16849v1)
- Classifier weights move first to a rank-2 cyclic configuration; embeddings subsequently align with them [ABS] — [arXiv:2606.08985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08985)
- "Frequency matching" between layers is explained by decoupled Fourier-mode dynamics [ABS] — [arXiv:2610.11833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11833)
- Final-state frequency agreement across tensors (RNN: same 6 frequencies in every tensor) [snippet] — [arXiv:2503.22059](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22059v1); readout using fewer frequencies than embedding in 5/10 seeds [REPO] — [Amarine007 repo](https://github.com/Amarine007/grokking-dynamics)

### Inferences
- F1 positioning, part by part:
  - "Embedding picks key frequencies early (epoch ~60–100), long before the jump (3,400–11,100)": variation on known. Closest prior: Amarine007 README (step 0–462 for 35/47 frequencies, 10 seeds, same setup) and Nanda et al.'s circuit-formation phase. Difference: user has 23 runs, a sharper and earlier timing estimate (60–100 epochs), and a quantified near-flat start (2–6% above median). Since the closest prior is a non-peer-reviewed repo, the claim is not established in the literature.
  - "Neurons become clean single-frequency (FVE>0.85) only near the jump": likely new as an explicit measurement. He et al. describe sparsification/pruning after memorization and single-frequency neurons at the end, but I found no time-resolved per-neuron FVE vs test-jump analysis, and no paper contrasting it with early embedding selection. The decoupling "global choice early, local commitment late" is the novel framing.
  - "1–2 extra frequencies become key and are later pruned (7/23 runs)": variation on known (Amarine007: frequencies dropped after circuit formation; He et al.: weight-decay pruning stage; Nanda: cleanup).
  - "Embedding vs output-side profile correlation 0.15 at init → ~0.9 by epoch 100–500": the time series itself appears new for transformers; theoretically anticipated (frequency matching in 2610.11833; phase coupling in 2602.16849; classifier-first ordering in 2606.08985). ## Q4. What determines which frequencies win? Prediction from init; per-neuron vs global selection; data split (positions F3)

### Takeaway
The only explicit "predict the winner from init" result is He et al. 2026's per-neuron lottery ticket (magnitude × phase alignment) in a 2-layer MLP, validated qualitatively. AGF (2025) predicts learning order by coefficient magnitude. In Nanda's transformer, the only measurement found (Amarine007 repo) shows a weak but significant init head start for W_E magnitudes (14/47 in top ten; mean rank 23.6 vs 28.5, p=0.016) and none for the readout. Dependence on the data split is theoretically implied (dynamics are driven by training-set statistics, 2610.11833) but I found no experiment that holds init fixed and varies the split.

### Cited Findings
- Per-neuron winner = largest initial input/output magnitudes and smallest phase misalignment (2-layer MLP, p=23; qualitative validation, no hit rate reported in the part read) — [arXiv:2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16849v1)
- Frequencies learned in decreasing order of coefficient magnitude (quadratic 2-layer, small init) [ABS] — [arXiv:2506.06489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06489)
- Growth conditions are set by training-distribution conditional statistics (probability signatures); decoupled per-mode dynamics [ABS] — [arXiv:2610.11833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11833)
- In 1-layer transformer (Nanda setup, fixed split, 10 inits): W_E init head start significant but small (p=0.016; 14/47 in top ten), W_L not significant (p=0.057); no causal planting experiment [REPO] — [Amarine007/grokking-dynamics](https://github.com/Amarine007/grokking-dynamics)
- Which group representations/frequencies are learned varies across seeds (weak universality) [MEM] — [arXiv:2302.03025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03025)
- Global balance: with enough neurons all frequencies are represented (p=23, M=512 → all 11) — [arXiv:2602.16849](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16849v1); roughly uniform survival across non-trivial frequencies for Z3×Z5 [snippet] — [arXiv:2606.02993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02993); max-margin uses Fourier features at single-frequency neurons [MEM] — [arXiv:2311.07568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07568)
- Number of features needed: O(log n) with trainable embeddings or depth >1 [ABS] — [arXiv:2505.18266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18266); transformers in Nanda's setup typically use ~5 key frequencies out of 56 [MEM] — [arXiv:2301.05217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217)

### Inferences
- F3 positioning:
  - "Per-neuron lottery score predicts the neuron's final frequency barely above shuffle (3–6% vs 2–4%)": closest prior is He et al. 2602.16849. Difference: they work in a 2-layer MLP with one-hot inputs (each neuron owns its own input weights per frequency), small init, p=23, mostly full data, and validate qualitatively; the user tests it quantitatively in a 1-layer transformer at default init, where neurons read a shared embedding through attention. A near-null result there is new and is a limitation/boundary result for the lottery-ticket mechanism, not a contradiction of it in its own regime.
  - "Summed input-side magnitude puts ~50% of final key frequencies in the top-10 (chance 18%)": closest prior is the Amarine007 README (14/47 ≈ 30% in top ten; p=0.016; readout not significant) and, theoretically, AGF's magnitude ordering. Difference: a stronger effect (≈2.8× chance vs ≈1.7×), and a global (summed over neurons) rather than per-neuron predictor. Variation on a known-but-unpublished result; new in the peer-reviewed sense.
  - "Phase alignment adds nothing on top of magnitude": new relative to He et al., whose rule gives phase misalignment an explicit role. Should be presented as "in the transformer at default init" since their theory assumes small init and quadratic activation.
  - "Same init, different data split → different key frequencies": no direct prior experiment found (Amarine007 and Nanda vary init with a fixed split). Theoretically expected: with full one-hot data the Fourier modes are symmetric up to the Z_p automorphism, so any asymmetry among frequencies must come from init or from the sampled 30% subset; 2610.11833's framework says the training distribution's statistics drive per-mode growth. Likely new as an empirical result.
- Together F1+F3 suggest a two-level picture: a global, early, partly init- and data-driven selection in W_E (and alignment with the output side), followed by late per-neuron commitment to already-chosen frequencies; per-neuron lottery tickets (He et al.) would then matter less in the transformer because the candidate frequency set is fixed upstream before neurons commit.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports a quantitative hit rate for any init-based frequency predictor (per-neuron or global) in a transformer.
- No paper found on how the training subset (which 30% of pairs) biases frequency selection; this is an open, testable question (e.g., compute the DFT of the train-set mask and correlate with winners).

## Q5. Pizza vs clock and other non-circular solutions; what controls which algorithm appears

### Takeaway
Zhong et al. 2023 showed transformers can learn "clock" (Nanda's multiplicative trig-identity circuit) or "pizza" (an additive, mean-of-embeddings circuit using |cos|-like features), controlled mainly by attention rate (uniform vs learned attention) and architecture, with mixed/parallel circuits common. 2025–2026 work argues these are geometrically/topologically equivalent or instances of one approximate-CRT algorithm, while ReLU MLPs can use square waves instead of sinusoids.

### Cited Findings
- Zhong, Liu, Tegmark, Andreas 2023 (NeurIPS 2023), "The Clock and the Pizza": small changes to hyperparameters/initialization yield qualitatively different algorithms; uniform-attention models learn pizza, learned attention learns clock; an "algorithmic phase space" over attention rate and width, with networks sometimes running several circuits in parallel; introduce "gradient symmetricity" and "distance irrelevance" diagnostics [MEM] — [arXiv:2306.17844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17844)
- Moisescu-Pareja, McCracken et al. (ICLR 2026): uniform-attention and trainable-attention architectures implement the same algorithm via topologically and geometrically equivalent representations; first layer learns torus or vector-addition-disc ("pizza") manifolds, later rotations/projections reach a logit annulus; the "diagonal dependence" thought to define pizza also appears in MLP-Add and Clock networks — [arXiv:2512.25060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.25060)
